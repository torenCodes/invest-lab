# The Fiber Backbone of AI: Why Optical Infrastructure Is the Sleeper Trade of the Decade

*Published March 2026*

---

Everyone knows the AI story. Nvidia prints money. Cloud hyperscalers spend hundreds of billions. Data centers multiply like rabbits. But beneath all that compute lies a quieter revolution — one that a sharp group of investors and analysts began flagging years before it became consensus: **the optical buildout**.

While the world was fixated on GPUs, a handful of voices on YouTube and X (formerly Twitter) were pointing at something less glamorous but equally critical: the cables, transceivers, and photonic components that actually move data between all those chips. They were early. They were right.

---

## Why Optics? Why Now?

Modern AI workloads — especially large language model training and inference — are fundamentally different from traditional data center tasks. Training a frontier model requires thousands of GPUs working in tight synchrony, passing enormous tensors back and forth constantly. The bottleneck isn't always compute. Often, **it's bandwidth**.

Optical interconnects solve this. Unlike copper, fiber can carry data at the speed of light with far lower latency and power consumption over distance. As AI clusters scaled from hundreds to tens of thousands of GPUs, the demand for high-speed optical transceivers, coherent modules, and photonic integration exploded — and it's still accelerating.

The numbers back this up. Hyperscalers like Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and Meta have each announced multi-hundred-billion-dollar infrastructure spending plans through 2027. A significant portion of that spend flows into optical networking — inside the data center (intra-DC), between data centers (inter-DC), and increasingly into the metro and long-haul networks being strained by AI traffic.

## The Companies Positioned to Win

Several publicly traded companies are at the center of this buildout, and a few have been discussed by forward-looking investors for years:

**II-VI / Coherent (COHR)** — The heavyweight. After the merger of II-VI and Coherent Corp, this company became one of the most vertically integrated optical component manufacturers in the world. They make everything from compound semiconductors to 400G and 800G transceivers to coherent modules. As data center speeds migrate toward 1.6T, COHR is deeply embedded in that roadmap. It's not a clean, simple story — integration has been bumpy — but the underlying demand thesis is hard to argue with.

**Lumentum (LITE)** — A precision optical and photonic products company with strong positioning in 3D sensing, telecom, and increasingly in data center optics. Lumentum's indium phosphide (InP) laser expertise is core to the high-speed transceivers that AI clusters demand. They've historically been volatile with telecom cycles, but the AI data center tailwind is a new and durable driver.

**AXT Inc. (AXTI)** — The picks-and-shovels play beneath the picks-and-shovels. AXT makes the compound semiconductor substrates — indium phosphide, gallium arsenide, germanium — that are the raw material for the lasers and photodetectors inside optical components. When COHR and LITE ramp production, AXTI benefits upstream. Small-cap, higher risk, but tightly correlated to optical growth without the integration complexity of larger players.

**Additional names worth watching:**

- **Fabrinet (FN)** — Contract manufacturer for optical components, meaning they assemble the finished transceivers for many of the names above. As optical volume scales, Fabrinet's highly automated Thailand facilities scale with it. Clean balance sheet, consistent execution.

- **Ciena (CIEN)** — The optical networking systems layer. Ciena's WaveLogic coherent technology powers long-haul and metro networks, and AI is driving unprecedented traffic growth outside the data center too. Less direct AI play than the component names, but a beneficiary of the same tailwind.

- **Applied Optoelectronics (AAOI)** — A smaller, more speculative name in data center transceivers. Higher volatility, but has seen meaningful demand upticks from hyperscaler relationships.

- **Marvell Technology (MRVL)** — Straddling semiconductor and optical silicon photonics, Marvell is developing custom silicon photonics solutions for hyperscalers that could reshape how optical components are integrated at scale. A larger-cap name for those wanting optical exposure with less pure-play risk.

## The Investment Case

The core thesis is simple: AI compute investment doesn't exist in a vacuum. Every GPU cluster needs to be networked. Every data center needs to connect to others. Every byte of AI-generated content needs to travel over fiber. **Optical is the connective tissue of the AI era.**

What makes this particularly compelling as an investment angle:

**Supply constraints are real.** High-speed optical transceivers — especially 400G, 800G, and the emerging 1.6T — require specialized manufacturing, compound semiconductors, and long qualification cycles with hyperscalers. Supply can't be conjured overnight. Companies that are already qualified and ramping have durable advantages.

**The upgrade cycle is long.** Data center optical infrastructure gets refreshed as speeds increase. The move from 100G to 400G was a multi-year cycle. The move to 800G and beyond is underway now. Each cycle requires new transceivers, new switches, new fiber plant — recurring demand, not a one-time event.

**Valuation has lagged the narrative.** While Nvidia trades at eye-watering multiples, many optical component companies still trade at relatively modest valuations despite secular tailwinds. As the AI infrastructure theme broadens beyond GPUs into the full stack, optical names may see multiple expansion.

---

## Risks to Acknowledge

No investment case is complete without honesty about the risks. Optical companies are historically cyclical — telecom spending booms and busts have burned investors before. Inventory digestion cycles can be painful. Integration execution risk (particularly at COHR) remains real. And if AI capex ever hits a wall, the whole infrastructure trade faces a re-rating.

But the structural case — that AI requires massive, sustained optical buildout — is well-supported by the spending commitments already locked in by the hyperscalers through the rest of this decade.

That kind of upstream supply chain digging — finding the company two levels beneath the headline names — is exactly the research that gets rewarded early.

**Martin Shkreli** has been one of the more vocal and unconventional voices in the optical space, publishing a detailed Substack essay on photonic computing and taking an advisory role at Q/C Technologies (QCLS). His thesis focuses on the longer-horizon possibility of photonics *replacing* GPUs for matrix multiplication entirely — a more speculative and futuristic angle than the transceiver/interconnect buildout discussed in this post, but part of the same broader conviction that light is the future of computing infrastructure. His willingness to make the argument publicly and loudly, regardless of how the mainstream received it, helped put the topic on more investors' radar.

That kind of bottom-up, public research work — supply chain digging, earnings call analysis, connecting upstream substrates to downstream hyperscaler capex — is how these ideas travel before they become consensus. If you found this thesis through any of these voices, you were early.
